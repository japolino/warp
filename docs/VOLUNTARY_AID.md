# Voluntary aid through ordinary chat

Implementation contract, 2026-11-04. This extends the shared engine, with no rulebook, genre preset, setup screen or external relay.

An NPC may offer an established beneficial ability to the player in actual narration. The offer is a choice, not an executed cure. The existing scene read prepares the NPC's ability and records its offer. The player can accept or decline in ordinary chat. Acceptance resolves one use by that NPC, with the NPC's prerequisites, resources, action clock and existing shared payer permissions. The player does not learn the power and granting permission does not consume a player action or tick a player affliction.

- A new offer cites the latest actual narrator, the actual actor, the player as target, one compiled ability/version, and explicit unresolved terms. Routine uncontested aid must actually be offered; a personality trait, the player's request or a hypothetical is insufficient.
- Keep source restrictions, costs and unknown recovery limits. A quoted price is not permission to omit payment; unsupported conditions keep that aid unresolved. State relevant costs with the offer, before acceptance, including a known shared payer.
- Acceptance authorizes only that offered beneficial use. No attack, extra responder, movement, other target, forced feelings, consent, new power or arbitrary actor plan. Harmful or mixed harmful/beneficial definitions are not aid. Special treatment limits remain binding.
- Validate presence, living target, actor capability, current ability version, range, conditions, cooldown, resources and treatment before any payment or consuming the offer. An unavailable action spends nothing and remains explicit. A supported successful execution consumes the offer once.
- One NPC use may advance that NPC's clock and due effects. It cannot create another response list. An incapacitated living player can accept aid; explicit death is not resurrection.
- An offer persists until used or explicitly withdrawn/declined. Old source quotations cannot reopen a used offer. Bare yes/please may be handled locally only when the immediately preceding narrator offers one unambiguous eligible aid and no competing payment/reward acceptance. Other messages use the ordinary bounded interpreter.
- Questions about effects/costs stay read-only. A request such as "Could you treat my arm?" may invite an NPC offer, never directly execute a player-owned spell or guarantee agreement. Ordinary dialogue remains usable when that aid is unsupported.
- Resolver 10 owns aid acceptance/decline. Resolvers 2 through 9 keep their former receipt paths and results. Stop, retry, swipes, reloads, branch edits and source changes retain existing transaction boundaries. Narrator prompt additions are appended, preserving earlier Warp blocks.

Verification covers offer-before-execution, voluntary source evidence, ownership, costs, treatment constraints, player/NPC timing, repeated acceptance, stale offers/definitions, cancellation/reload/branch replay, quiet informational questions, prefix stability, actual worker execution and isolated original-card play. A passing schema or fixture is not proof of a seamless or enjoyable first session.

### Interceptor deadline correction

The fresh fbd4eac pilot still narrated a cure. Its retained outgoing request contained no Warp block at all, and its player message had no saved Warp prompt. In contrast, earlier completed requests did contain their saved Warp blocks. The local host's interceptor setting is ten seconds; its current worker context does not supply interceptorDeadlineAt, while Warp's old fallback allowed up to twelve seconds for interpretation after other work. A timed-out interceptor can therefore lose both mechanical preparation and the narrator boundary.

Warp now budgets eight seconds from interceptor entry on that host and reserves 1.5 seconds to publish its prompt and unresolved status. A runtime test with a deliberately non-returning helper completes the interception in 6.5 seconds, retains aid/preparation guidance, saves the current prompt and spends nothing. Explicit smaller host deadlines still constrain the budget. This does not make the model faster, prove every provider is reliable, or complete original-card acceptance testing.
